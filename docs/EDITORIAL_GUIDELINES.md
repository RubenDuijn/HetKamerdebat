# EDITORIAL GUIDELINES — HetKamerdebat

## Purpose and scope
This handbook is a practical operating guide for editors and writers.
It complements, and does not replace:
- EDITORIAL_POLICY.md (positioning and standards)
- EDITORIAL_TEMPLATE_SYSTEM.md (metadata, templates, safeguards)
- CONTENT_STRATEGY.md (content architecture)

Use this file for day-to-day editorial decisions: format selection, research workflow, and publication readiness.

## Format selection: when to use each format

### 1. NEWS
Use NEWS when there is a new, verifiable political development:
- a decision, announcement, parliamentary event, vote, motion, amendment, letter, or official document

Core objective:
- explain what happened, who was involved, when it happened, and what happens next

Editorial rule:
- separate confirmed facts from attributed statements, allegations, and pending claims


### 2. POLITICAL ANALYSIS
Use POLITICAL ANALYSIS when the key task is explanation:
- why a political development happened
- how power, party positions, negotiations, and institutional choices shaped the outcome

Core objective:
- explain mechanisms and consequences, not only chronology

Editorial rule:
- clearly distinguish evidence from interpretation


### 3. BACKGROUND
Use BACKGROUND when readers need structural context:
- institutions, legal frameworks, legislative procedures, historical context, or policy architecture

Core objective:
- provide durable reference material that improves understanding of current affairs

Editorial rule:
- do not use BACKGROUND as a substitute for current political reporting


### 4. POLITICAL ESSAY
Use POLITICAL ESSAY / POLITIEKE BESCHOUWING when the article investigates long-term political trajectories:
- how earlier decisions, omissions, warnings, promises, or predictions relate to later developments

Core objective:
- present a clear thesis, historical evidence, alternative explanations, and a reasoned conclusion

Editorial rule:
- write in engaging, essayistic Dutch while preserving strict evidence discipline


## Editorial identity and verification boundaries
HetKamerdebat has a recognisable pro-FVD editorial orientation and gives specific attention to FVD ideas, proposals, warnings, and analyses.

This orientation never overrides verification:
- do not assume FVD is automatically correct
- verify political claims against primary sources and independent evidence
- examine both supporting and contradictory evidence
- distinguish party positions from established facts
- report whether predictions were correct, partly correct, incorrect, or unverifiable
- do not present correlation as proof of causation
- disclose editorial orientation transparently in public-facing context


## Mandatory research workflow (applies to every article)
1. Define the central news value or research question.
2. Collect primary sources first.
3. Verify dates, quotations, figures, and political positions.
4. Identify relevant opposing arguments and alternative explanations.
5. Separate facts, attributed statements, analysis, and editorial conclusions.
6. Record uncertainties and missing information explicitly.
7. Include a source register and claim-to-source mapping.
8. Require human editorial approval before publication.

No publication is allowed if critical claims cannot be verified to a defensible editorial standard.


## Practical decision guide

### Quick format picker
Choose NEWS if the lead is event-first.
Choose POLITICAL ANALYSIS if the lead is mechanism-first.
Choose BACKGROUND if the lead is context-first.
Choose POLITICAL ESSAY if the lead is long-term thesis-first.

### Concrete examples
- NEWS: A ministry publishes a new migration measure and parliamentary debate is scheduled.
- POLITICAL ANALYSIS: Coalition behavior shifts the expected outcome of that migration measure.
- BACKGROUND: Explainer on how migration legislation moves from proposal to implementation.
- POLITICAL ESSAY: Multi-year review of migration warnings, policy choices, and measurable outcomes.

### When NEWS may include POLITICAL ANALYSIS elements
A NEWS article may include limited analytical context if it improves clarity.
Keep the main format as NEWS when all conditions are true:
- the primary value is the new development itself
- analysis is brief and explicitly labeled as interpretation
- speculative claims are avoided
- readers can still clearly separate facts from interpretation

Escalate to POLITICAL ANALYSIS when interpretation becomes the dominant value of the piece.


## Publication readiness checklist
Before handoff for approval, confirm:
- format choice is explicit and defensible
- factual claims are source-backed
- uncertainty block is complete where relevant
- counterarguments are included when needed
- source register and claim mapping are complete
- human editorial approval status is set to required or approved
